Moot Court

Advocacy, argument and legal strategy.

Bringing legal reasoning into practice through research, written submissions and oral advocacy.

The Programme

From legal analysis to persuasive argument.

Corporate Law Academy’s Moot Court programme gives students practical exposure to advocacy through simulated legal proceedings.

Participants examine a dispute, develop a legal position and present arguments in writing and orally. The process connects academic knowledge with the demands of representing a party and defending its case.

Core Activities

Research. Write. Advocate.

Legal Research

Analyse the facts, identify the legal issues and assess the relevant authorities. Develop a case theory that accounts for both the strengths and weaknesses of your position.

Written Submissions

Translate research into clear, structured pleadings. Build a persuasive line of reasoning, support it with precise references and address the opposing party’s arguments.

Oral Advocacy

Present your case, respond to questions from the bench and adapt your submissions under time pressure. Communicate complex legal arguments with clarity and discipline.

Skills & Development

Judgment beyond the classroom.

Mooting requires more than identifying the applicable law. It involves deciding which arguments to advance, which points to concede and how to respond when a position is challenged.

Preparing a case also develops teamwork: coordinating research, reviewing submissions and building a consistent position together. These skills extend beyond advocacy to legal writing, negotiation and professional communication.
